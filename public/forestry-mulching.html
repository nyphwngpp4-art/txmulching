What is forestry mulching?

Forestry mulching is land clearing with a single machine that cuts, grinds and spreads brush and trees as mulch in one pass. A rotating drum with carbide teeth reduces everything from briars to hardwood stems to wood chips. The chips stay on the ground as a protective layer, so there's nothing to pile, burn or haul off.

It replaces the old three-step routine of dozing, piling and burning. That means less soil disturbance, less cost and a usable property sooner.

How much does forestry mulching cost in East Texas?

Every property is different, so we quote each job instead of publishing a price list. Brush density drives most of the cost: the thicker the growth, the more machine time the work takes. Light brush you can walk through costs far less to clear than a wall of hardwood timber. Send a quick request or call, and we'll talk through your acreage, brush and location.

What changes the price?

  • Density and stem size. Big-diameter hardwood takes longer to grind than yaupon and saplings.
  • Ground conditions. Rock, steep slopes and wet or soft ground slow the machine down.
  • Debris hauling. Mulching in place is included; hauling material off-site is not.
  • Access and travel. Distance from Canton and how easily the machine can reach the work.

Most jobs get a quick site walk before we quote, and the number is firm once we give it.

Is forestry mulching worth it on a small lot?

Usually not with our equipment. Under about 3 acres, a compact skid-steer mulcher is usually the more economical choice, and we'll tell you that up front. Our machine pays off on heavy timber and larger acreage.

How many acres can a forestry mulcher clear in a day?

A high-horsepower forestry mulcher like ours clears multiple acres a day, even in thick East Texas timber. A compact skid-steer mulcher may manage only a fraction of an acre a day in the same conditions. That gap is why a bigger machine often costs less per job: 10 acres of heavy brush at one acre a day bills five times the machine-days.

Is forestry mulching better than bulldozing?

Forestry mulching is better when you want to keep your topsoil, keep the trees you like and skip burn piles. Bulldozing is better when you need stumps and roots pulled and bare dirt graded, such as for a building pad or a pond. Mulching grinds stumps down to about ground level but doesn't dig out roots.

  • Choose mulching for pasture recovery, hunting land, trails, fence lines, underbrush control, right-of-way and clearing around homesites.
  • Choose dirt work for foundation pads, pond digging, grading and full root removal. TX Mulching handles that too: the owner runs excavation equipment himself, so clearing and dirt work can be one job with one point of contact.

What's left after forestry mulching?

A layer of wood mulch across a clean, walkable surface. The mulch holds soil in place against erosion, holds moisture, slows regrowth and breaks down into the ground over time. Selected trees can be left standing: tell us which ones to keep and we'll clear the understory around them.

What can forestry mulching clear?

Nearly any overgrowth a landowner wants gone in East Texas, including yaupon, privet, Chinese tallow, cedar and hardwood thickets. Common jobs include:

  • Overgrown pasture and acreage reclamation
  • Fence-line clearing and new fence routes
  • Trail, road and hunting-lane cutting
  • Homesite and lot clearing
  • Right-of-way, utility and road-edge corridors
  • Pond edges and blocked access routes
  • Underbrush control that keeps the good trees standing

Forestry mulching questions

Do you burn or haul off the brush?

No. Everything is mulched in place and left as ground cover. Hauling debris off-site is available if you need it.

Can you keep certain trees?

Yes. Selective clearing is one of forestry mulching's main advantages. Mark or point out the trees you want kept and we'll work around them.

Can you handle excavation, stump removal or pond digging?

Yes. The owner has 50+ years on heavy equipment and does excavation and dirt work himself, and he has contacts for specialized machines when a job calls for them. Mulching by itself grinds stumps to about ground level; if you need stumps and roots pulled or the ground dug and shaped, we can do that as part of the same job.

Do you work commercial and government jobs?

Yes. We've logged 250+ days of Texas Parks & Wildlife contract work and take commercial and right-of-way projects.
